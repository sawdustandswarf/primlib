15 mm rail housing

primlib/primitives/rail15_housing.py · class Rail15Housing · all primitives

The receiver for a rail15: a block block_width (y) by block_depth (z) by length (x) with the slot cut along it, through both ends. It shares the rail's four fixed parameters, and its depth must match the rail's. The full dimensioned specification, with the working, is rail15_spec.html.

The rail has no tolerance, so the fit is all here: the slot is the rail grown by clearance, normal to every flat face. At the floor the radius is tip_radius + clearance, concentric with the rail's. The slot's widest point is therefore 15 + 2 x clearance.

The mouth's lip is sharp at the default clearance. Its radius is root_radius - clearance, concentric with the rail's root radius, which is 0 at a clearance of 0.20 and can't be less. So from 0.20 up the lip is a sharp corner, chosen when the part is built; below that it's rounded. Either way the gap stays clearance: rebuild after moving the clearance across 0.20.

The frame is the rail's, not the block's: the housing is centred on y = 0 and runs along x from 0 to length, with the rail's root at z = 0, so its mating face is at z = -clearance and its floor at z = -depth - clearance. A rail15 built at the same origin sits in it, mated, whatever the clearance.

end_taper is an option, 0 (off) by default: lead-ins at both ends of the slot. Over the last end_taper of its length the slot grows by end_taper, normal to its faces, so at 45 degrees along x; the mating face stays where it is. Between the lead-ins the profile is exact, and that's where to measure the mouth. The choice of on or off is fixed when the part is built: rebuild after changing it.

printer_width_offset is added to the slot's width, half to each side, across y only, and is the housing's own value: a printer needn't move a slot as it moves a rail. The block's outside doesn't change. With different offsets the two models don't show the printed gap, and may overlap as models.

For an assembly, body() returns the block and, as a cut tool, the slot, reaching 1 above the face and past both ends.

Shape

Fit with 15 mm rail

A rail slid in, built at the same origin: the 15 mm rail takes this part's values of length, depth, end_taper, printer_width_offset; its own parameters keep their defaults. Both are cut across at x = 10, halfway along.

Check of the two sections: no interference. Minimum clearance 0.2 mm, at e5.

Use

import primlib
# appended to the document's Params sheet under "Rail15Housing: 15 mm rail housing",
# aliases Rail15Housing_clearance, Rail15Housing_length, ...
obj = primlib.make("rail15_housing", doc, "Rail15Housing")
obj = primlib.make("rail15_housing", doc, "Rail15Housing", clearance='0.20', length='20')   # starting values

# into another sheet, or with plain shared aliases (clearance, ...)
obj = primlib.make("rail15_housing", doc, "Rail15Housing", sheet=sheet)
obj = primlib.make("rail15_housing", doc, "Rail15Housing", prefix="")

# or drive params from expressions you already have
obj = primlib.make("rail15_housing", doc, "Rail15Housing", sheet=sheet,
                 bind={"clearance": "MyAlias * 2"})

In the FreeCAD GUI: the primlib workbench has a button for 15 mm rail housing. Its parameters are appended to the document's Params sheet under a title row, or to the selected spreadsheet if one is selected.

Objects created: the spreadsheet rows, the hidden helper objects described above, and the result <name>, tagged PrimlibPrimitive = "rail15_housing". They are plain FreeCAD objects: the saved file does not need primlib.
