Stencil

primlib/primitives/stencil.py · class Stencil · all primitives

Two templates for putting text on a board: up to six lines, each with its own font, size, place and justification, cut through a plate that lies on the board's face, registered by fences that hook over the board's edges, each switched on or off. Trace the letters with a pencil, paint through them, or rout them with a bearing-guided bit.

The letters' centres (the middle of O, A, B, 4, 8 ...) are held by thin bridges across each line, as long as the line. Template A has its bridges at bar_a1 and bar_a2 of each line's capital height, template B at bar_b1 and bar_b2, so each covers what the other's bridges hide: go over the letters with A, then again with B. Both register on the same fences, so they line up. With a stencil font, set bridges to 0 and use either. The result is a compound of {name}_a and {name}_b.

Frame: the origin is the template's centre, on the board's face (z = 0), with the board below it. The plate lies on the face from z = 0 to thickness, length along x and width along y, fences included; the text reads along +x, its tops towards +y.

Text: lines lines (1 to 6) are placed from the text's origin, (origin_x, origin_y) from the template's centre, the centre itself by default. Line i's anchor is (line{i}_x, line{i}_y) from the origin; by default the lines are stacked down, text_size + line_gap apart, centred on the origin. h{i} sets where the anchor is along the line (0 left, 1 centre, 2 right) and v{i} where it is up the capitals (0 top, 1 middle, 2 the baseline). Each line's font{i} and size{i} follow font and text_size unless set.

Fences: fence_top, fence_bottom, fence_left and fence_right (1 on, 0 off) put a fence under the plate along that edge, fence_t thick, reaching fence_depth down the board's edge, clearance off it. Push the template against the fenced edges: two fences on adjacent sides locate it on a corner; one alone sets only its distance from that edge, as for text in the middle of a large board. The text must stay on the board, inside the fences (x_lo to x_hi, y_lo to y_hi).

Router: the bearing rides the template's walls, so thickness must be at least the bearing's height, every stroke at least bit_d wide (the routed letters' inside corners are rounded to bit_d / 2), and each bridge of A at least bridge_w + bit_d from each of B's, so B can rout what A's bridges hid.

Print each template top face down: the fences stand up, and nothing overhangs. The text, fonts and sizes are sheet cells: edit them and recompute. The build can't check what it can't see before the text is drawn: a line running off the plate's ends, and a letter whose centre the bridges don't hold (it shows as a separate solid in its template). The default bridge heights hold the capitals and digits of the default font.

Use

import primlib
# appended to the document's Params sheet under "Stencil: Stencil",
# aliases Stencil_length, Stencil_width, ...
obj = primlib.make("stencil", doc, "Stencil")
obj = primlib.make("stencil", doc, "Stencil", length='160', width='120')   # starting values

# into another sheet, or with plain shared aliases (length, ...)
obj = primlib.make("stencil", doc, "Stencil", sheet=sheet)
obj = primlib.make("stencil", doc, "Stencil", prefix="")

# or drive params from expressions you already have
obj = primlib.make("stencil", doc, "Stencil", sheet=sheet,
                 bind={"length": "MyAlias * 2"})

In the FreeCAD GUI: the primlib workbench has a button for Stencil. Its parameters are appended to the document's Params sheet under a title row, or to the selected spreadsheet if one is selected.

Objects created: the spreadsheet rows, the hidden helper objects described above, and the result <name>, tagged PrimlibPrimitive = "stencil". They are plain FreeCAD objects: the saved file does not need primlib.
