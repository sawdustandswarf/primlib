Rotary latch

primlib/primitives/rotary_latch.py · class RotaryLatch · all primitives

A rotary latch for a box lid: a hex knob on the body reaches over the lid joint and turns over an arc of 15 mm rail on the lid. Five parts, each its own object in the result compound ({name}_base, _knob, _cap, _lid, _bridge).

Frame: the case surface is z = 0, the knob's axis is z (out of the case), y is up towards the lid and x across; the pin's axis is the origin.

Base (on the body): a plate plate_w wide from plate_bot up to the gap at the joint, four countersunk M3 at x = ±screw_x, the pin (as big as fits: wall_in of knob between its bore and the groove, with a 45 deg root and a pilot hole for the cap screw) and two V ribs the knob's flat back runs on, cut off at the plate's edge.

Knob: one piece, a hexagon knob_af across the flats. Its back carries the rail15_housing slot at clearance, revolved all round about the pin at radius rail_r, with one notch through the slot's outer wall at 12 o'clock, notch_x either side, where the rail drops in. Each side of the notch is a lead-in ramp: over ramp_a the slot's outer lip comes in from pull_down further out to true (an eccentric copy of the slot, ecc off the axis, kept to the ramp's sector and outside rail_r). The ramps mirror, so the knob locks turned turn either way, and a lid sitting up to pull_down high is drawn down. Printed front face down: the slot's flanks lean 30 deg. Held on the pin by the cap and an M3 into the pilot.

Lid (on the box lid): a plate plate_w wide and lid_h high with four countersunk M3 lined up with the base's, carrying the rail15 profile revolved over rail_span at radius rail_r, its support cut off at the plate's lower edge. It is built where it is mounted: preload above the locked position, so turning the knob pulls it in by that much (preload - clearance of it against the parts, which must flex) and pull_down - preload of the ramps is left for tolerance.

Bridge: a print aid, one layer (bridge_t) across the gap between the plates and bridge_lap under each, so the two plates print as one piece in their mounted places. Screw both on, then cut it along the joint.

The fixed parameters are the rail's (rail15).

Use

import primlib
# appended to the document's Params sheet under "RotaryLatch: Rotary latch",
# aliases RotaryLatch_knob_af, RotaryLatch_knob_len, ...
obj = primlib.make("rotary_latch", doc, "RotaryLatch")
obj = primlib.make("rotary_latch", doc, "RotaryLatch", knob_af='56', knob_len='11')   # starting values

# into another sheet, or with plain shared aliases (knob_af, ...)
obj = primlib.make("rotary_latch", doc, "RotaryLatch", sheet=sheet)
obj = primlib.make("rotary_latch", doc, "RotaryLatch", prefix="")

# or drive params from expressions you already have
obj = primlib.make("rotary_latch", doc, "RotaryLatch", sheet=sheet,
                 bind={"knob_af": "MyAlias * 2"})

In the FreeCAD GUI: the primlib workbench has a button for Rotary latch. Its parameters are appended to the document's Params sheet under a title row, or to the selected spreadsheet if one is selected.

Objects created: the spreadsheet rows, the hidden helper objects described above, and the result <name>, tagged PrimlibPrimitive = "rotary_latch". They are plain FreeCAD objects: the saved file does not need primlib.
