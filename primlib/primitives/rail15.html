15 mm rail

primlib/primitives/rail15.py · class Rail15 · all primitives

Four fixed parameters define its shape, and aren't for changing: width 15, angle 60, tip_radius 1 and root_radius 0.2. Its variable parameters are length, depth, base_depth, base_width and printer_width_offset. The rail has no tolerance. The full dimensioned specification, with the working, is rail15_spec.html.

The width is 15.00 by design: everything else is worked from it, and it is measured where calipers measure it, between vertical tangents to the tip radii. It doesn't depend on depth or on the root.

The rail runs along x from 0 to length, centred on y = 0, its root at z = 0, its tip at z = -depth, and its support above the root. A rail15_housing built at the same origin sits around it, mated.

The root radius is always there and always runs its full turn, into the support's underside, so the support can't be left out: base_depth must be more than 0. With base_width -1 the support is as wide as the root including its radius, so the radius ends exactly at the support's edge. Otherwise base_width must be wider than that. Whether base_width is negative is fixed when the part is built: rebuild after changing its sign.

end_taper is an option, 0 (off) by default: lead-ins at both ends, so the rail finds its way into a housing. Over the last end_taper of its length the tapered part shrinks by end_taper, normal to its faces, so at 45 degrees along x, the tip radius going down to match. The support doesn't change. Between the lead-ins the profile is exact, and that's where to measure the width. The choice of on or off is fixed when the part is built: rebuild after changing it.

printer_width_offset is added to the width in the model, half to each side, across y only, so the depth, angle and radii stay exact. It isn't part of the fit. To set it, print at 0, measure the width, and use 15 - measured.

Shape

Fit with 15 mm rail housing

The rail slid into a housing built at the same origin: the 15 mm rail housing takes this part's values of length, depth, end_taper, printer_width_offset; its own parameters keep their defaults. Both are cut across at x = 10, halfway along.

Check of the two sections: no interference. Minimum clearance 0.2 mm, at e5.

Use

import primlib
# appended to the document's Params sheet under "Rail15: 15 mm rail",
# aliases Rail15_length, Rail15_depth, ...
obj = primlib.make("rail15", doc, "Rail15")
obj = primlib.make("rail15", doc, "Rail15", length='20', depth='4')   # starting values

# into another sheet, or with plain shared aliases (length, ...)
obj = primlib.make("rail15", doc, "Rail15", sheet=sheet)
obj = primlib.make("rail15", doc, "Rail15", prefix="")

# or drive params from expressions you already have
obj = primlib.make("rail15", doc, "Rail15", sheet=sheet,
                 bind={"length": "MyAlias * 2"})

In the FreeCAD GUI: the primlib workbench has a button for 15 mm rail. Its parameters are appended to the document's Params sheet under a title row, or to the selected spreadsheet if one is selected.

Objects created: the spreadsheet rows, the hidden helper objects described above, and the result <name>, tagged PrimlibPrimitive = "rail15". They are plain FreeCAD objects: the saved file does not need primlib.
