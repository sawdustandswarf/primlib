Topeak rail

Specification of topeak_rail and topeak_rail_housing · rail page · housing page · all primitives

The width is 61.40, what the print should measure. A PETG piece modelled at 61.6 printed at 61.4 and fits a real rack, a whisker tight, so the rail's default printer_width_offset is 0.20. Sizes and drawings on this page are the design size, offset 0, unless a row says otherwise. Eight fixed parameters define the shape, and the rail has no tolerance. All sizes are in mm.

2. The rail

3. Why the width is exactly 61.40

A radius tangent to the tip face and the flank has its centre t in from the sharp corner, along the tip face, set by half the 60° angle. Its vertical tangent is one radius outside its centre. So half the width is the sharp corner, less t, plus the radius. The primitive turns that round to get the sharp corner from the width:

t = tip_radius / tan(angle / 2) = 0.7 / tan 30° = 1.212436 tip_hw = width / 2 + t - tip_radius = 30.70 + 1.212436 - 0.7 = 31.212436 check, worked back from the sharp corner: 2 x (tip_hw - t + tip_radius) = 2 x (31.212436 - 1.212436 + 0.7) = 61.400000

Substituting, t and tip_radius cancel, leaving width. Neither depth, the root nor the groove enters it. The primitive refuses to build if the check differs from the width by a millionth of a mm.

4. The support, drawn

5. The clearance and the matching part

clearance (default 0.30) is the gap between the printed parts. It's normal to every face and per side, so a width across the slot grows by twice it. The slot is the rail grown by it and the guide is the groove shrunk by it. The tip's radius keeps its centre and grows to 0.70 + clearance. The mouth's lip keeps the root radius's centre, with radius 1.00 - clearance.

A real rack isn't made to these numbers. This housing is the printed mate, for testing and for parts of our own. The rail's fit on a real rack is found by trying it: see section 8.

6. Tapered ends

end_taper is an option on both parts, off by default: lead-ins, so the rail finds its way in rather than catching on the end. It's the same size on both ends of a part. Over the last end_taper of the length, the tongue shrinks by end_taper normal to its faces, its groove opening by the same, and the housing's slot grows by it, its guide shrinking. The change is the same as the distance along x, so the lead-in is at 45°.

The rail's support doesn't change, nor does the housing's mating face. Between the lead-ins, both parts have exactly the profile shown above, so the width and the mouth are measured there. At the very end the rail's tip radius is 0.70 - end_taper and the slot's floor radius is 0.70 + clearance + end_taper, each concentric with the full radius, and the root radius and the lip stay as they are. So end_taper must be less than 0.70.

7. The printer width offset

This keeps the printer out of the fit. clearance is only ever the gap between the printed parts; whatever the printer does to a part's width is corrected separately, on that part. Every size in this document is the design size, what the print should measure.

width in the model = design width + printer_width_offset

Half goes to each side, across y only: the flanks move sideways, unchanged, so the depth, the angle and the radii stay exact. The groove and the guide stay at their design size and the flat tip face and floor take up the difference. Each part has its own value, and the block's outside doesn't change.

rail: printer_width_offset = 61.40 - (width measured at 0) housing: printer_width_offset = (design mouth, section 5) - (mouth measured at 0) this rail, PETG, standing on end: at model 61.60, measured 61.40 -> 0.20 under, so offset 0.20 model = 61.40 + 0.20 = 61.60, should print 61.40

The rail's default offset is that 0.20. The housing's default stays 0: no slot has been printed and measured yet. For another printer or filament, print the rail at offset 0 and use the formula above.

A negative offset is allowed. If the two offsets differ, the two models won't show the printed gap, and may even overlap as models. That's expected: it's the printed parts that have the clearance.

8. Where the width came from, and measuring a print

topeak_rail_print_test.stl in the claude project has seven pieces at model widths 60.4 to 62.2 in 0.3 steps. In PETG, standing on end, the one modelled at 61.6 fitted a real rack best, a whisker tight, and measured 61.4. That print is the design width. The working below ties it back to the rack by the 1 : √3 : 2 triangle: a caliper reading at a rounded corner of radius r is 0.732 r per side from the sharp corner, and the flank runs 6.35 / √3 = 3.666 per side over the depth.

rack, sharp mouth = mouth reading - 2 x 0.732 x mouth radius rack, sharp floor = sharp mouth + 2 x 3.666 rack, widest = sharp floor - 2 x 0.732 x floor radius with the mean mouth reading 57.19, and one radius r at the mouth and floor: rack, widest = 57.19 + 7.33 - 2.93 r a rack of about 61.5, just over the print, gives r = 1.03

That radius is plausible, but it's a consistency check, not a measurement of the rack.
