Hinge

primlib/primitives/hinge.py · class Hinge · all primitives

The library's hinge: VerticalHinge3 (and 3b, the same hinge 13 long with 2 rings and 7.5 side plates), the hinge printed and confirmed for the hybrid case. Print in place, standing on end: two sides of interleaved rings on a pin, each side with a leaf to screw to a panel.

The axis runs along z from 0 to length. Side A's leaf runs along +x and side B's along -x; their mounting faces are at +y, pin_d / 2 + pin_gap + ring_wall + backset from the axis. Rings are length / rings apart, side A's the first and every other one. The pin is a stack of flared slices, one per ring. Each leaf is cut clear of the other side's rings over half a turn, so the hinge rolls from folded flat to open flat.

The leaves' square edges carry a 45 degree chamfer, cut flat into the box, so it holds as the hinge resizes. side_b defaults to side_a; set it shorter where side B's leaf has to clear something (the T hinge under a corner).

The result is a compound of three solids, {name}_side_a, {name}_side_b and the pins (labelled {name}_pins), so a project can place the sides apart.

Use

import primlib
# appended to the document's Params sheet under "Hinge: Hinge",
# aliases Hinge_length, Hinge_rings, ...
obj = primlib.make("hinge", doc, "Hinge")
obj = primlib.make("hinge", doc, "Hinge", length='40', rings='4')   # starting values

# into another sheet, or with plain shared aliases (length, ...)
obj = primlib.make("hinge", doc, "Hinge", sheet=sheet)
obj = primlib.make("hinge", doc, "Hinge", prefix="")

# or drive params from expressions you already have
obj = primlib.make("hinge", doc, "Hinge", sheet=sheet,
                 bind={"length": "MyAlias * 2"})

In the FreeCAD GUI: the primlib workbench has a button for Hinge. Its parameters are appended to the document's Params sheet under a title row, or to the selected spreadsheet if one is selected.

Objects created: the spreadsheet rows, the hidden helper objects described above, and the result <name>, tagged PrimlibPrimitive = "hinge". They are plain FreeCAD objects: the saved file does not need primlib.
