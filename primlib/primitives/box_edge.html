Box edge

primlib/primitives/box_edge.py · class BoxEdge · all primitives

One length of the edge along which two panels of a box meet. The section is an L: a skin over each panel's outside, a web panel_gap wide between the panels' edges, and a bevel across the outside corner. The panels drop into the two rebates and are screwed through the skin.

Glue line: with glue above 0, the faces that hold the panels are cut back by glue, leaving two ridges glue_ridge wide on each face, along the edge. The panels sit on the ridges, exactly where they sit with no glue, so no other size changes, and the pockets between them are the glue line. They're open at both ends, so glue and air can get out. Their sides are at 45 degrees.

Relief: with relief above 0, the -x wall's outside face is cut back by relief over the whole section and length, for something to pass beneath it, with the small chamfers carried round the new face. holes_x and holes_y turn either screw row off (0), for a wall left too thin for the countersinks, say.

Frame: the inside corner at the origin, the walls towards -x and -y, the edge along +z from 0 to length. Each panel's inner face is on x = 0 or y = 0.

The small chamfer is part of the shape, not a feature picked from its edges, so nothing can lose it when a size changes. Its faces are on every outward square edge along the section and all round both ends.

Two rows of countersunk holes go through the skins, one per panel, on the line panel_gap + drill_inset from the inside corner, from drill_inset from one end to drill_inset from the other, at no more than nominal_spacing.

Use

import primlib
# appended to the document's Params sheet under "BoxEdge: Box edge",
# aliases BoxEdge_length, BoxEdge_panel_t, ...
obj = primlib.make("box_edge", doc, "BoxEdge")
obj = primlib.make("box_edge", doc, "BoxEdge", length='100', panel_t='6.5')   # starting values

# into another sheet, or with plain shared aliases (length, ...)
obj = primlib.make("box_edge", doc, "BoxEdge", sheet=sheet)
obj = primlib.make("box_edge", doc, "BoxEdge", prefix="")

# or drive params from expressions you already have
obj = primlib.make("box_edge", doc, "BoxEdge", sheet=sheet,
                 bind={"length": "MyAlias * 2"})

In the FreeCAD GUI: the primlib workbench has a button for Box edge. Its parameters are appended to the document's Params sheet under a title row, or to the selected spreadsheet if one is selected.

Objects created: the spreadsheet rows, the hidden helper objects described above, and the result <name>, tagged PrimlibPrimitive = "box_edge". They are plain FreeCAD objects: the saved file does not need primlib.
