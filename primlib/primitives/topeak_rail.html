Topeak rail

primlib/primitives/topeak_rail.py · class TopeakRail · all primitives

The tongue that slides in a Topeak rack. Eight fixed parameters define its shape, and aren't for changing. Its variable parameters are length, base_depth, base_width, end_taper and printer_width_offset. The rail has no tolerance. The full dimensioned specification, with the working, is topeak_rail_spec.html.

The width is 61.40, what the print should measure. A PETG piece modelled at 61.6 printed at 61.4 and fits a real rack, a whisker tight. So the default printer_width_offset is 0.2, and the default model is 61.6.

The width is measured where calipers measure it, between vertical tangents to the tip radii, and everything else is worked from it.

The rail runs along x from 0 to length, centred on y = 0, its root at z = 0, its tip at z = -depth, and its support above the root. A topeak_rail_housing built at the same origin sits around it, mated.

The root radius always runs its full turn, into the support's underside, so the support can't be left out: base_depth must be more than 0. With base_width -1 the support is as wide as the root including its radius, so the radius ends exactly at the support's edge. Otherwise base_width must be wider than that. Whether base_width is negative is fixed when the part is built: rebuild after changing its sign.

end_taper is an option, 0 (off) by default: lead-ins at both ends. Over the last end_taper of its length the tongue shrinks by end_taper, normal to its faces, so at 45 degrees along x, the groove opening with it and the tip radius going down to match. The support doesn't change. The choice of on or off is fixed when the part is built: rebuild after changing it.

printer_width_offset is added to the width in the model, half to each side, across y only, so the depth, angle and radii stay exact. The groove doesn't move. For another printer or filament, print at 0, measure the width, and use width - measured.

Shape

Fit with Topeak rail housing

The rail slid into a housing built at the same origin: the topeak rail housing takes this part's values of length, end_taper, printer_width_offset; its own parameters keep their defaults. Both are cut across at x = 10, halfway along.

Check of the two sections: no interference. Minimum clearance 0.3 mm, at e10.

Use

import primlib
# appended to the document's Params sheet under "TopeakRail: Topeak rail",
# aliases TopeakRail_length, TopeakRail_base_depth, ...
obj = primlib.make("topeak_rail", doc, "TopeakRail")
obj = primlib.make("topeak_rail", doc, "TopeakRail", length='20', base_depth='2')   # starting values

# into another sheet, or with plain shared aliases (length, ...)
obj = primlib.make("topeak_rail", doc, "TopeakRail", sheet=sheet)
obj = primlib.make("topeak_rail", doc, "TopeakRail", prefix="")

# or drive params from expressions you already have
obj = primlib.make("topeak_rail", doc, "TopeakRail", sheet=sheet,
                 bind={"length": "MyAlias * 2"})

In the FreeCAD GUI: the primlib workbench has a button for Topeak rail. Its parameters are appended to the document's Params sheet under a title row, or to the selected spreadsheet if one is selected.

Objects created: the spreadsheet rows, the hidden helper objects described above, and the result <name>, tagged PrimlibPrimitive = "topeak_rail". They are plain FreeCAD objects: the saved file does not need primlib.
